## Post 1 — TODAY: Medicaid changes need navigators

**Article:** Healthbeat, Nov 3, 2026 — "Medicaid work requirements: States, community groups expand outreach on rule changes"
https://www.healthbeat.org/2026/11/03/medicaid-changes-spark-community-outreach-to-keep-people-covered/

**Post:**

Medicaid work requirements start by January 1, 2027 — and states are already funding navigators to help people keep their coverage.

This hits home. For 13+ years I helped people through the U.S. healthcare system, and most recently I worked on Medicaid long-term care applications. What I learned: people rarely lose coverage because they aren't eligible. They lose it because of paperwork — a missing document, a deadline nobody explained, a form in a language they don't read.

That's exactly where navigators and community health workers make the difference.

If your organization is building Medicaid outreach for Spanish- or Portuguese-speaking communities, I'd love to connect.

## Post 3 — TOMORROW: AI + care navigation (news)

**Article:** HIT Consultant, Nov 6, 2026 — "Ellipsis Health acquires Kouper Health" (AI voice agent + post-discharge care navigation)
https://hitconsultant.net/2026/11/06/ellipsis-health-acquires-kouper-health-ai-care-management-ehr-navigation/

**Post:**

AI is moving into care navigation. Ellipsis Health just acquired Kouper Health, which uses AI voice and text agents to follow up with patients after they leave the hospital.

As a patient navigator who uses AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude) in my own daily work, here's my take: AI is great at reminders, scheduling, and paperwork. It saves hours.

But the hardest calls I've made weren't about scheduling. They were with a scared patient, in Spanish, who didn't trust the system. That part still needs a human.

The future I'd bet on: AI handles the routine, navigators handle the people. Navigators who know how to use AI will do both better.

What do you think — where should AI stop and humans take over in patient care?
