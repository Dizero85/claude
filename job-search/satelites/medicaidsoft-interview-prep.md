## Medicaid Long-Term Care — Key Facts to Know

### Basic Eligibility (nursing home)
- Age 65+ OR permanently disabled OR blind
- Must need **Nursing Facility Level of Care (NFLOC)** — proven medical need
- Must be a resident of the state where applying
- Income and assets must be under state limits

### Financial Limits (2026)
| | Individual |
|--|--|
| **Income limit** | $2,982/month (most states) |
| **Asset limit** | $2,000 (most states) |

### Spousal Protections
- Non-applicant spouse keeps up to **$162,660** in assets (CSRA — Community Spouse Resource Allowance)
- Non-applicant spouse income allowance: **$2,643–$4,066/month** (MMNA — Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance)

### Exempt Assets (never counted)
- Primary home (if spouse or child under 21 lives there)
- One vehicle
- Personal belongings and furniture
- Irrevocable funeral trusts
- Some life insurance policies

### The Look-Back Period — Critical Concept
- **60 months (5 years)** — state reviews ALL financial transactions before application
- Assets given away or sold below market value = **penalty period** (months of ineligibility)
- Penalty = transferred amount ÷ state's average daily nursing home rate
- Exception: California (30 months), New York

### Spend-Down — How people qualify when over the limit
Legal ways to reduce assets:
- Pay off debt
- Home modifications / medical equipment
- Irrevocable funeral trust
- Caregiver agreements
- Annuities (with careful planning)

## Quick Glossary (in case they test you)

| Term | What it means |
|------|--------------|
| NFLOC | Nursing Facility Level of Care — medical need requirement |
| CSRA | Community Spouse Resource Allowance — what the healthy spouse can keep |
| MMNA | Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance — income the healthy spouse keeps |
| Look-Back Period | 60-month window the state reviews for asset transfers |
| Spend-Down | Legal process of reducing assets to qualify |
| Penalty Period | Months of Medicaid ineligibility due to improper asset transfers |
| Exempt Assets | Assets that don't count toward the limit |
