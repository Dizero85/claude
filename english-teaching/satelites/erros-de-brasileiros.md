# The Brazilian Speaker's Error Guide

> Companion to [`livro-do-programa.md`](livro-do-programa.md). These are the mistakes Portuguese speakers make most, with the fix and a quick drill for each.
> **Teacher tip:** don't teach this as a list. Use it to *recognize* errors during free talk, then pick the 3–5 that matter most for each student.

---

## 1. Pronunciation — the "Big 8"

| # | Problem | What Brazilians do | Fix | Drill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Extra "i" at the end** | *big-i, like-i, Facebook-i, hot-dog-i* | Stop the sound. Close the mouth / hold the tongue. | *big, book, like, stop, good, work, nice, Brazil* |
| 2 | **TH** | *"tank you"*, *"fank you"*, *"sree"* | Tongue lightly between the teeth, blow air | *think, three, thank, Thursday, third, mouth · the, this, that, mother* |
| 3 | **Initial R / H** | *rice → "hice"*; *hotel → "rotel"* (with Portuguese R) | English R: tongue curled back, doesn't touch anything. English H: just soft air | *red/head, rice, right, Rio · house, hotel, happy, help* |
| 4 | **Final -ED** | *work-ed, play-ed, ask-ed* | 3 sounds: /t/ worked · /d/ played · /id/ only after t/d: wanted, needed | *I asked, I liked, I stopped · I called, I stayed · I visited, I waited* |
| 5 | **Vowel length** | *ship = sheep, live = leave, full = fool* | Long vowels are LONG and tense; short ones relaxed | *sit/seat, live/leave, fill/feel, pull/pool · beach, sheet, piece* (keep long!) |
| 6 | **Final M / N** | *"time"* sounds like *"taym"*, *"I'm"* like *"aim"* nasal | Close the lips for M, touch tongue on top for N | *time, name, home, come · ten, fine, phone, train* |
| 7 | **Initial S + consonant** | *"es-tudent", "es-pain", "es-tarbucks"* | Start directly with S — no "e" before | *student, Spain, Starbucks, school, stop, speak, small* |
| 8 | **Final L** | *"Brazil" → "Braziu", "hotel" → "hoteu"* | Tongue touches behind the top teeth at the end | *Brazil, hotel, call, feel, people, little* |

**Also:** word stress (*HO-tel* ✗ → *ho-TEL*; *de-VE-lop*; *COM-for-ta-ble* = "COMF-ter-bul"); "I" sound in English is often "ai" (*idea = ai-DI-a*), and many silent letters (*walk, talk, half, Wednesday, listen, island*).

## 3. "Translated" sentences — the grammar traps

| ❌ Brazilian translation | ✅ Natural English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| I have 60 years. | **I'm 60.** / I'm 60 years old. | Age uses BE |
| I am agree. | **I agree.** | Agree is a verb |
| I'm with hunger / cold / headache. | **I'm hungry / cold.** **I have a headache.** | |
| Do you want that I help you? | **Do you want me to help you?** | |
| She said me… | **She told me…** / **She said (that)…** | tell + person |
| I lost the bus / the flight. | **I missed the bus / my flight.** | |
| I'm going to make a question. | **I'd like to ask a question.** | ask a question |
| I made a party / a trip. | **I had a party.** **I took a trip.** | |
| People is nice. | **People are nice.** | People = plural |
| I live here since 2010. | **I've lived here since 2010.** | Teach as chunk |
| It depends of… | **It depends on…** | |
| I'm thinking in going. | **I'm thinking about going.** | |
| Explain me… | **Explain to me…** / **Can you explain…?** | |
| I need go. / I want go. | **I need to go. / I want to go.** | |
| He don't like… | **He doesn't like…** | 3rd person |
| Is very beautiful. | **It's very beautiful.** | English needs a subject |
| Have many people here. | **There are a lot of people here.** | "Tem" = there is/are |
| I stayed in the house. | **I stayed home / at home.** | |
| Do you like to have a coffee? | **Would you like a coffee?** | invitation |
| How do you call this? | **What do you call this?** / How do you say…? | |
| I'm boring. | **I'm bored.** | "boring" = chato (causes) |
| What means ___? | **What does ___ mean?** | question order |
| Where is the bathroom? (US) | **Where's the restroom?** | works, but restroom is more natural in public places |
| I want a beer. | **Could I get a beer, please?** | "I want" can sound rude |

## 4. Conversation habits (cultural)

- **Too direct requests** ("Give me…", "I want…") → soften with *Could I…? / Can I get…? / I'd like…*. In English, politeness lives in the grammar.
- **Too long answers to officials** → in immigration and interviews, short and direct. Brazilians tend to explain too much when nervous.
- **Not saying "please", "thank you", "sorry", "excuse me"** often enough — English speakers use them constantly.
- **Interrupting / speaking at the same time** — normal in Brazil, can seem rude in the US/UK. Practice waiting for the end of the sentence.
- **Physical closeness / touching** — in many English-speaking countries, people keep more distance with strangers.
- **"Yes" when you didn't understand** — the most dangerous habit. Always: *"Sorry, could you repeat that?"*
